Why Circuit Saver shifts, not cuts

Circuit Saver's shed/restore mechanism primarily changes when your energy is used, not how much. The economic case doesn't depend on lowering your total kWh — it depends on flattening the narrow peak-demand window that would otherwise justify building an expensive new power plant.

A peaker plant is sized for the top 5% of demand hours in a year. If enough households shift load out of that narrow window, the plant is never needed — regardless of total annual consumption. Secondary savings also come from avoiding rebound-spike inefficiency and shifting consumption toward hours with a cleaner generation mix.

How Circuit Saver decides

Technical depth

1. Predict

A Random Forest Regressor estimates your transformer's normal ("baseline") load for the current hour and weather, so any shed request can be measured against a real reference point — not a guess.

2. Allocate

The Swarm Engine — a deterministic optimizer, not a model — distributes a shed target across connected households by a transparent priority score: 40% carbon weighting, 40% cost weighting, 20% flexibility.

3. Protect

An Anti-Rebound Classifier staggers everyone's restart time (0–45 min) so devices don't all switch back on simultaneously and cause a second spike. Transformer loading is the single strongest input to that decision.
